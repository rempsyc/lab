**Wanting to help and carrying out useful help are different outcomes.** The intention–behavior gap asks why an endorsed goal does not always become an action. For prosociality, a further question is whether the action actually benefits its recipient.

This primer connects [measurement](/concepts/measuring-prosocial-behavior/) with the SAGE Lab’s proposed [robust prosociality framework](/concepts/robust-prosociality/).

## Distinguish the targets

A benevolent aspiration is a broad wish for others to fare well. A behavioral intention concerns a specified action, recipient, and occasion. An ultimate motive concerns the goal served by acting. These are different claims: an intention to donate does not alone establish why the person intends to donate.

Consider someone who intends to support an overwhelmed colleague. They may never notice a practical opportunity, lack time or skills, forget the intention, or offer assistance the colleague does not need. Each calls for a different explanation.

## A guide to locating the obstacle

| Question | Possible obstacle | What to record |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Was there a relevant opportunity? | No need, request, access, or feasible option | Exposure, resources, and available alternatives. |
| Was the opportunity recognized? | Inattention or mistaken interpretation | Recognition separately from the action. |
| Was a goal or intention activated? | Competing goals, weak commitment, or changed priorities | Episode-specific intention and reasons. |
| Was a response carried out? | Forgetting, fear, practical difficulty, or lack of skill | Actual behavior, timing, and costs. |
| Did the response help? | Poor fit, incorrect assumptions, or changed needs | Recipient feedback and meaningful consequences. |

This is a design aid, not an established compulsory sequence. People may act habitually without reporting a fresh intention, and action or feedback can change subsequent motivation.

## What existing evidence supports

[Webb and Sheeran (2006)](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.132.2.249) synthesized experiments that changed intentions and assessed subsequent behavior. Intention changes were accompanied by smaller behavioral changes. This supports separating the outcomes; it does not provide a universal gap size for prosociality.

Implementation intentions link a relevant cue to a planned response: “If this situation occurs, then I will do this.” The [Sheeran, Listrom, and Gollwitzer synthesis (2025; online 2024)](https://doi.org/10.1080/10463283.2024.2334563) finds benefits across diverse outcomes and identifies conditions that moderate them. Planning is a candidate tool for enactment, not a guarantee of effective or enduring helping.

Self-determination theory distinguishes personally endorsed action from externally pressured or internally compelled action. Autonomous motivation includes valued activities that are not enjoyable. [Ryan and Deci (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cedpsych.2020.101860). Research on helping also examines the quality of motivation and helper and recipient well-being. [Weinstein and Ryan (2010)](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0016984).

## Motivation and implementation need different tests

To test stronger motivation, assess the relevant goal or concern. To test enactment, provide or observe a feasible opportunity and assess what happens. To test effectiveness, measure the recipient’s outcome.

**Illustrative plan:** “When I notice that a colleague is overloaded, I will ask which specific task would be useful to take on.” This prepares information seeking, rather than prescribing the same act every time. Whether it helps in a given setting remains an empirical question.

Training can target commitment, noticing, planning, skill, or the environment. A useful study names the proposed obstacle and measures it independently. Do not interpret any positive intervention effect as proof of the preferred mechanism.

## Avoid misleading comparisons

Measure intentions before the relevant action and specify the time window. Match the intention’s specificity to the behavior: a broad wish to benefit humanity is a poor stand-in for an intention to volunteer tomorrow.

Repeated observation can separate typical differences between people from changes within a person. Treat no opportunity, inability, refusal, and missing reports separately. If training changes which opportunities are noticed, conditioning only on noticed opportunities can produce a selected comparison. A sequential pattern or mediation estimate alone does not establish a causal chain.

A comparison of experienced practitioners with nonpractitioners cannot isolate the causal effect of training from selection and other differences. Random assignment to specified training components addresses a narrower causal question.

## Connection to robust prosociality

A dependable response may require personally endorsed concern and learned enactment, with appropriate opportunities and support. This is part of the lab’s proposed account, not a conclusion established by the studies above. Persistence, transfer, recipient effectiveness, and helper sustainability require their own outcomes.

**Essential reading:** Webb and Sheeran for experimental intention change; Sheeran et al. for planning; Ryan and Deci for motivational distinctions; Weinstein and Ryan for helping-specific motivation and well-being.
