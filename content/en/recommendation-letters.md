A strong letter gives the reader a concrete example of how you work. A professor might describe how you revised a paper after feedback, noticed a problem in an analysis, or followed through on a difficult project. Those details help a committee see what you could bring to its program.

## Give someone something to write about

- **Do well in your courses.** Strong grades give a professor evidence of academic preparation. Keep a piece of work you’re proud of, especially if it shows how you reasoned through a problem or improved after feedback.
- **Make your thinking visible.** Ask a specific question after class, visit office hours to discuss an idea, or follow up on feedback you used. A few substantive conversations can help a professor connect your name to the way you think.
- **Build research experience where you can.** A supervised project, internship, or research assistantship can show how you handle uncertainty, communicate, and follow through. A course project can also provide a strong example. Keep track of what you did personally, especially in group work.

## Choose your referees

Read the program’s instructions first. Check how many letters it asks for, who can write them, and which qualities it wants assessed. Choose people who have seen relevant work firsthand. A professor who supervised your project may have more to say about your research readiness than a prominent professor who only knows your grade. For a clinical program, a supervisor who observed your work in a helping role may also be a good choice if the program accepts that kind of reference.

## Ask clearly and early

Ask several weeks before the first deadline when possible. Say what you’re applying for and ask whether the person feels able to write a **strong, specific letter**. Give them room to decline. A short message can be enough:

> Hello Professor [Name],  
> I’m applying to [program], and the letter is due [date]. I took [course] with you in [term] and wrote [brief reminder of the work]. Would you feel comfortable writing a strong letter in support of my application? I’d be happy to send my CV, the program criteria, and examples of my work. I understand if your schedule or familiarity with my work makes this difficult. Thank you for considering it.

Once they agree, send the deadlines and submission instructions in one place. Include your CV, transcript or draft statement if helpful, and a short reminder of work they actually saw. Mention any prompts the committee asks referees to address. If the portal shows the letter as pending, send a brief reminder a few days before the deadline. Thank your referee afterward and let them know the outcome.
