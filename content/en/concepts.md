These primers introduce concepts used in SAGE Lab research. Each connects definitions and examples with situational influences, individual differences, major theories, measurement, and open questions.

## A guided reading path

1. [The science of prosocial behavior](/concepts/prosocial-behavior/) maps the questions, constructs, predictors, and major theories.
2. [Measuring prosocial behavior](/concepts/measuring-prosocial-behavior/) compares tasks and reports, and explains what each can establish.
3. [Persons and situations](/concepts/persons-and-situations/) examines who helps whom, under which conditions.
4. [Empathy and compassion](/concepts/empathy-and-compassion/) distinguishes understanding, emotional sharing, concern, and distress.
5. [Altruism](/concepts/altruism/) examines other-oriented motivation and its alternatives.
6. [From prosocial intention to action](/concepts/intention-to-action/) follows the path from concern and goals to behavior and recipient outcomes.
7. [Robust prosociality](/concepts/robust-prosociality/) introduces the lab’s proposed framework for dependable, adaptive helping.

Read in order for a field overview, or start with the question relevant to a study. The methods and theory pages link back to one another.

## Robust prosociality

Our [proposed framework for robust prosociality](/concepts/robust-prosociality/) asks how concern becomes dependable, effective helping across time, challenges, settings, recipients, and costs. It distinguishes personally endorsed concern from learned ways of acting, including the ability to revise help when needs change.

The framework and its seven proposed dimensions are under development. **Robust prosociality** takes a broad view of beneficial behaviour, including altruistic, self-interested, and mixed motives.

Use the [measurement guide](/concepts/measuring-prosocial-behavior/) when choosing outcomes and the [persons and situations guide](/concepts/persons-and-situations/) when specifying conditions and predictors.
