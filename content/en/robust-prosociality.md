**A proposed SAGE Lab framework.** We use *robust prosociality* as a working name for beneficial helping that remains dependable across specified changes in time, demands, settings, recipients, or personal costs, while allowing sustainable participation. The name, boundaries, and explanatory model remain under development.

The central question is: **how can concern for others become dependable action when circumstances become less supportive—or when the familiar way of helping no longer works?**

## Why robustness matters

A sincere intention, a generous choice in one task, and effective help over time are different achievements. In this proposal, robustness concerns a pattern of behaviour under named conditions. It can describe a person’s helping in a particular domain, an intervention’s effects, or the support provided by a social system.

Dependability does not mean repeating an action regardless of its consequences. If a recipient’s needs change, reliable helping may require a different response, asking for guidance, or referring to someone better equipped. Limits and recovery can support continued participation; unlimited self-sacrifice is not the criterion.

## Seven proposed dimensions

| Dimension | What we would test |
| --- | --- |
| Persistence | Does helping or an intervention effect endure over a justified period, including after support is withdrawn? |
| Resilience | Does it remain dependable under a specified challenge, such as distraction or time pressure? |
| Generalization | Does it transfer to meaningfully different situations, tasks, or recipients? |
| Cost tolerance | How does behaviour change as actual costs in time, effort, or resources increase? |
| Translation into action | Does concern or intention lead to observable action when a feasible opportunity exists? |
| Recipient effectiveness | Does the intended beneficiary actually benefit? |
| Helper sustainability | Can participation continue without unacceptable burden or harm to the helper? |

These dimensions may dissociate. A routine can persist while becoming ineffective; willingness to accept greater immediate costs can undermine future participation. We therefore propose reporting a **profile of outcomes and conditions**, rather than assuming a single trait or adding the dimensions into an unvalidated score.

Behavioural robustness also differs from robustness of scientific evidence. Dependable helping needs direct observation; reliable measurement, defensible analyses, and appropriate samples support the conclusions drawn about it.

## A candidate explanation

Our current working account distinguishes two processes:

- **Motivational integration:** concern is personally endorsed, potentially reducing the recurrent need to persuade or pressure oneself to help.
- **Learned enactment:** practice makes it easier to notice opportunities, retrieve feasible responses, and carry them out. Learning may produce a fixed routine or a flexible procedure for selecting and revising help.

This account draws on existing work. Self-determination theory distinguishes autonomous endorsement from external pressure or internal compulsion; an activity can be freely valued without being enjoyable. [Ryan and Deci, 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cedpsych.2020.101860). Autonomous helping has also been studied in relation to helper and recipient well-being. [Weinstein and Ryan, 2010](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0016984). Moral expertise provides an antecedent for connecting learning, perception, and skilled action. [Narvaez and Lapsley, 2005](https://maplab.nd.edu/assets/224606/narvaez_lapsley_psychological_foundations_of_everyday_morality_and_moral_expertise.pdf).

Our proposed synthesis is that cultivation may reduce the effort needed to motivate oneself to help **while preserving the thought needed to work out what would help**. Identity may support endorsement or shape whose needs are noticed; it is not assumed to be a separate necessary ingredient. Opportunity, information, resources, and institutional support remain part of the explanation.

## A testable contrast

Imagine two training approaches that initially produce similarly fluent, beneficial helping. One teaches a familiar action; the other teaches how to choose a response using information about the recipient’s needs. Now change which action benefits the recipient.

The proposal predicts that learning an adaptive procedure should support more appropriate switching than learning only the fixed act, under conditions where the new information can be understood and used. Measure both the response and its benefit. If the approaches perform equivalently with adequate measurement and precision, that would challenge the proposed distinction in that setting.

This is a candidate test, not an established finding. Whether the account adds explanatory value beyond existing motivation, habit, and expertise theories remains an open question.

## Robust prosociality or robust altruism

The naming decision remains open. *Robust prosociality* accommodates beneficial behaviour with altruistic, self-interested, or mixed motives. *Robust altruism* would make the narrower motivational claim that another person’s welfare is the ultimate goal; dependable behaviour alone would not establish that claim.

For now, robust prosociality is the working label. We have not established a validated scale, a universal moral trait, or the novelty of the term or model.
